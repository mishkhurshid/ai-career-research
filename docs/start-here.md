# Start Here: Choose Your AI Career

If you're an NJIT student exploring AI jobs, you **do not need to decide on a career today**. Use this guide to shortlist two roles, read real employer descriptions, and choose one portfolio project to try.

## Step 1 — What work sounds most interesting?

| I enjoy... | Explore first | What the work often looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coding **and** meeting customers to solve their problems | [Forward Deployed Engineer](/roles/forward-deployed-engineer) | Requirements conversations, integration, deployment |
| Building features in AI-powered applications | [AI / Applied AI Engineer](/roles/) | Backend, APIs, model integration, testing |
| Designing tool-using automated workflows | [AI Agent Engineer](/roles/) | Orchestration, safeguards, evaluation |
| Working deeply with language models and retrieval | [LLM Engineer](/roles/) | RAG, model evaluation, retrieval quality |
| Designing systems across teams and technologies | [AI Solutions Architect](/roles/) | Architecture, security, cost, stakeholder planning |
| Making AI systems reliable after launch | [AI Platform / MLOps Engineer](/roles/) | Deployment, monitoring, infrastructure |

**Tip:** These titles overlap. Always compare actual responsibilities rather than relying on the title alone.

## Step 2 — Check the career level

Read [Internships and Jobs by Level](/job-levels) to see what employers ask of interns, new graduates, experienced engineers, and senior engineers.

If you're currently a student, **start with internships and new-graduate descriptions**, then use senior roles to understand a possible long-term direction. Confirm graduation-year eligibility, work location, and application deadlines with the employer.

## Step 3 — Ask yourself five questions

1. Would I rather build an AI product or customize one for a customer's needs?
2. Do I enjoy explaining technical ideas to people who don't code?
3. Would I rather develop a new feature or maintain a system that already runs?
4. Do I like experimentation and measuring model quality?
5. Am I willing to learn cloud deployment, security, and testing—not just prompting?

## Step 4 — Choose a project

Go to the [Student Portfolio Roadmap](/portfolio-roadmap) and pick one project. The best evidence includes code, a working demonstration, tests, documentation, and a short explanation of why you made technical decisions.

## Step 5 — Record a tentative decision

Copy these prompts into your notes:

- **Role I'm exploring:** 
- **Why it interests me:** 
- **One employer posting I read:** 
- **Two skills I already have:** 
- **Two skills I need to develop:** 
- **One project I could build this semester:** 

You can change your mind. This is career exploration, not a commitment.

## Example student journey

A hypothetical NJIT student who likes Python, business problems, and talking with people might shortlist **Forward Deployed Engineer** and **Applied AI Engineer**. They could try one client-style AI workflow project and one product feature, then compare which experience they enjoy more.
